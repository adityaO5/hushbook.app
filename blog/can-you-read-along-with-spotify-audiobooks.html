Can You Read Along With Spotify Audiobooks? Page Match, Recaps and Transcripts

Published October 27, 20268 min read

Key takeaways

  • Not on screen. Spotify does not show a book's text while an audiobook plays. Its help pages and newsroom posts describe no transcript, synced text or word highlighting for audiobooks.
  • Page Match, launched in February 2026, lets you scan a page of your own print book or e-book in the Spotify mobile app to jump to that spot in the audiobook, or to find your place in the book after listening.
  • Page Match works on iOS and Android. It started with most English-language titles, and in April 2026 Spotify said it was adding more than 30 more languages. Premium and Audiobooks+ members use it within their listening hours; free users can use it with audiobooks they bought.

  • For word-by-word highlighting, HushBook transcribes free public domain audiobooks and files you import, such as MP3 and M4B, on your phone. For a book you hear on Spotify, Page Match with your own copy is the way to read along.

Not on screen. Spotify doesn't show a book's text while the audiobook plays. Its help pages and newsroom posts describe no transcript, no synced text and no word highlighting for audiobooks. The transcripts you may have seen in the app belong to podcasts.

What Spotify has instead is Page Match, launched in February 2026. You scan a page of your own print book or e-book with your phone's camera, and Spotify finds that spot in the audiobook. It works the other way too, telling you where to pick up reading after a stretch of listening. So you can read along on Spotify, but you bring the text and keep your own place.

If what you wanted was the words on screen, lighting up as the narrator says them, Spotify doesn't do that. HushBook, the app behind this blog, does, for free public domain audiobooks and audio files you own. Here is what Spotify does offer, how to read along with it anyway, and where each option stops, all from Spotify's own pages.

Can you read along with Spotify audiobooks?

Yes, if you bring the book. Spotify has three features that touch on reading, and none of them puts the book's text on your screen:

  • Page Match syncs your place between the audiobook and your own print or e-book copy. Details below.
  • Recaps are short spoken summaries of what you've heard so far.
  • Follow-Along, which Spotify described as a test in November 2024, shows "time-synchronized illustrations, photos, and graphics" on select titles, in the audiobook's extras. Pictures, not text.

So reading along on Spotify means holding a book or e-reader while the app plays. That suits plenty of people. It is just more manual than an app that highlights each word for you. Audible has one of those, Read & Listen, if you own both the Audible audiobook and the Kindle ebook. Our guide to reading along with Audible covers it.

Do Spotify audiobooks have text or transcripts?

No. Spotify's "Unlock Audiobooks" help page lists what the Now Playing view gives you on an audiobook: play speed, skip forward or back 15 seconds, a sleep timer, a rating once you've heard a chapter, and sharing. There is no text. Spotify's help center has no article on audiobook transcripts, and none of the audiobook announcements we read, from November 2024 to October 2026, mention one.

The mix-up is easy to make. Podcasts and audiobooks sit side by side in the same app, and many podcast episodes do show their words as they play. That is a podcast feature, covered further down.

Wanting the text isn't fussy. Against listening alone, seeing the words helps: a 2012 meta-analysis of 57 studies by Adesope and Nesbit found that people who got spoken and written words together outperformed people who only heard them. And in a 2013 study of 211 students, listening led to more mind wandering than reading aloud or reading silently. Whether it is worth the effort depends on the book, and our guide to whether reading along helps goes through when it does.

What is Spotify Page Match?

Page Match moves you between a book and its audiobook using your phone's camera. Spotify announced it on February 5, 2026, as a feature "that lets readers seamlessly switch between the printed (or e-book) and audiobook versions of a title."

What Spotify's pages say about it:

  • Devices: the Spotify mobile app on iOS and Android, using the phone's camera. Spotify's posts don't mention desktop.
  • Books: at launch, "most English-language titles." In April 2026 Spotify said it was expanding to more than 30 additional languages, including French, German and Swedish. The Page Match button only appears on supported titles.
  • Plans: Premium subscribers and Audiobooks+ members use it within their monthly audiobook listening hours. Free users can use it with audiobooks they've bought on Spotify.
  • Countries: Spotify's posts list languages and devices for Page Match, not countries. Audiobooks themselves were in 22 markets in April 2026, and on October 7, 2026, Spotify said it is expanding them to more than 180 by the end of the year. For the hours each plan includes, see how to listen to audiobooks for free.
  • Page numbers: the October 2026 post added them: "Simply scan the book's ISBN barcode and use page numbers to find your place." By then, Spotify said, more than one million readers had used Page Match.

Page Match matches a page you are holding to a moment in the audio. The words stay in your book.

How to read along on Spotify audiobooks

You need the audiobook on Spotify and your own copy of the same book, in print or on an e-reader. If you don't have one, Spotify has sold print books through Bookshop.org inside its app in the US and UK since April 2026. Then:

  1. Match the editions. Use the unabridged audiobook and the full text, or the two drift apart. An abridged recording skips passages your book still has.
  2. Find your spot with Page Match. Open the audiobook in the Spotify mobile app, tap the Page Match button, then Scan to listen. Scan the page you're on, and Spotify offers Play from here or Save for later. If it can't match the page, it asks you to try again.
  3. Set the speed to your reading. ACX, Audible's production platform, says "most performers narrate about 9,300 words per hour," roughly 155 words a minute. A 2019 meta-analysis by Marc Brysbaert puts adult silent reading in English at 238 words a minute for non-fiction and 260 for fiction. So 1.25x or 1.5x often brings the voice closer to your eyes. One catch on Premium: Spotify counts your listening hours at normal speed, so a 5-hour book uses 5 hours even if you finish it at 2x.
  4. Follow by eye. Nothing highlights, so run a finger or your eyes along the line with the voice, and pause when you fall behind.
  5. Switch back to the page. When you stop listening, tap Page Match, choose Scan to read, and scan any page. Spotify tells you whether to flip forward or back to reach the passage where you stopped.

What are Spotify audiobook Recaps?

Recaps are short audio summaries of the part of a book you've already heard. Spotify launched them in beta on November 13, 2025, on iOS, for "a limited selection of English-language titles," and brought them to Android in April 2026.

  • Tap the Recap button at the top of an audiobook's page.
  • The first Recap is ready after the first 15 to 20 minutes of listening, then updates as you go.
  • Recaps "only cover the portion of the book you've already heard," so they don't spoil later chapters.
  • Spotify says Recaps "use AI technology," and that it is "not using audiobook content for LLM training purposes or voice generation."

On titles that have both, Page Match and Recaps work together. A Recap is spoken, not written, so it helps you rejoin a story, not read it.

Do Spotify podcasts have read along?

Many do. In September 2023, Spotify began rolling out "auto-generated and time-synced transcripts" to more shows "so you can read along with an episode." On your phone, scroll down the Now Playing view to the transcript and tap it to follow along full screen. On desktop, Spotify's help lists podcast transcripts in the Now Playing view.

Not every show has them. Spotify's help for creators puts it plainly: "Some shows have auto-generated episode transcripts." Creators can turn them off for an episode or a whole show, and transcripts aren't offered to every creator. So if one episode shows text and another doesn't, that's down to the show, not your app.

How to read along word by word instead

Page Match keeps your place, but it can't put the words in front of you or show which one the narrator is on. For that you need an app that lines the text up with the recording.

That's what HushBook does. It transcribes an audiobook on your phone and highlights each word as the narrator reads it. Choose the Page reading mode and the text sits on a book page that turns as the narration moves on. Start with the free public domain audiobooks from LibriVox and the Internet Archive built into the app, thousands of classics read by volunteers, or import your own files: MP3, M4B, AAC, OGG, FLAC or WAV.

A few details:

  • What's free. Transcription, word highlighting and the free catalogue cost nothing. Pro adds unlimited e-book syncs and extras such as reading insights, goals and a dictionary.
  • Your e-book, if you have one. If you own a DRM-free EPUB, PDF or TXT of the same book, HushBook can sync it to the audio so the e-book's own text is highlighted instead of a transcript. E-book sync works with English narration, and your first sync is free.
  • On your phone. Transcription runs entirely on the device, and listening can start while the rest of the book transcribes.
  • Jump by line. Long-press any line to play from there.

Spotify audiobooks play only inside Spotify, and Spotify's user guidelines forbid "ripping" and "transferring copies of cached Content from an authorized Device to any other Device." So the two fit together: for a book you hear on Spotify, Page Match with a print or e-book copy is your way to read along, and for the free classics and the audio files you own, such as MP3 and M4B, HushBook puts every word on screen.

Honest limits

  • Spotify changes its features often. Everything here comes from Spotify's support pages and newsroom posts, checked on October 27, 2026. Its posts announce features but rarely say when a test ends, so we don't know whether Follow-Along is still running.
  • "No text for audiobooks" rests on what Spotify's pages leave out: no help article on audiobook transcripts, and no mention in the announcements we read. We did not check every title or app version.
  • We did not test Page Match or Recaps. Spotify's pages don't say how reliably a scan matches, including a scan of an e-reader screen.
  • Spotify's posts say which languages and devices get Page Match, not which countries.
  • The reading-along research mostly uses short passages in labs and classrooms. None of it tests Spotify, Page Match or any particular app.

No. Spotify's help pages and newsroom posts describe no transcript, synced text or word highlighting for audiobooks. Transcripts on Spotify are a podcast feature.

Do Spotify podcasts have transcripts?
