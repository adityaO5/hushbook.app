What is the best audiobook app for dyslexia?

For most adults, HushBook, because it highlights every word as a human narrator reads it, with no account and no eligibility form. The others cover narrower cases:

  • Books you own, read by a person, with every word on screen: HushBook, free to download, with a reading profile set up for dyslexia.
  • Textbooks read by people: Learning Ally, if you have a documented print disability.
  • A text-to-speech library, if you qualify: Bookshare, read in Bookshare Reader or Dolphin EasyReader.
  • PDFs, work documents and web pages: Speechify or Voice Dream Reader.
  • New bestsellers with the words highlighted: Audible Read & Listen, if you buy the Kindle ebook too.
  • Free library audiobooks: Libby.

The research points the same way. A 2018 meta-analysis of 22 studies by Wood and colleagues found that read-aloud tools modestly improved reading comprehension for students with reading disabilities, and the Yale Center for Dyslexia and Creativity advises teachers to "recommend that students read along with the audiobook version or to follow the words alongside the reader." We go through those studies in are audiobooks good for dyslexia.

What is the best dyslexia app for adults?

For an adult reading books, HushBook. Learning Ally and Bookshare serve adults too, but charge a yearly fee ($135 and $79.99) and need a professional to confirm a print disability first. HushBook asks for nothing: no account and no eligibility form. Import the audiobook files you own, such as MP3 and M4B, and each word lights up as the narrator says it.

For new releases, Audible Read & Listen works if you own both editions of a book; for work PDFs, Speechify or Voice Dream Reader read them in synthetic voices. If attention is part of the picture too, see are audiobooks good for ADHD and how to focus on audiobooks.

Is there a free app for dyslexia?

Yes. These apps have a free way in:

  • HushBook is free to download. Transcription and read-along are free for every book you add, and thousands of public-domain classics from LibriVox and the Internet Archive are built in.
  • Libby is free with a library card: "no subscription costs, no in-app purchases, and no late fees."
  • Bookshare is free for qualified US students, through an award from the US Department of Education.
  • Dolphin EasyReader is a free app that downloads "up to five titles at a time" from connected libraries.
  • Speechify has a free plan with 10 "robotic sounding voices" and speeds up to 1.5x.

Learning Ally has no free adult plan, but offers a financial assistance form.

Audiobooks or text-to-speech: which helps more with dyslexia?

The research does not pick a winner. Wood and colleagues coded whether each study in their meta-analysis used synthesized text-to-speech, a live human reader or a recorded human voice, and the only factor that significantly changed the results was study design. So choose by what you read:

  • A human-narrated audiobook for novels, memoirs and long non-fiction, where you hear the narrator's own pacing and character voices.
  • Text-to-speech for anything that has no audiobook: PDFs, work reports, articles and many textbooks.

HushBook covers the audiobook side and shows the words for the narrator you chose, at any speed from 0.5x to 3x. For text-to-speech tools, see our Speechify alternatives and our free alternative to Speechify for human-narrated audiobooks.

Which apps highlight each word as the narrator reads?

Three of the seven do it with a human narrator:

  • HushBook, for any audiobook file you import and for the built-in classics. It makes the word-by-word text from the recording itself, right on your phone.
  • Learning Ally, on its VOICEtext titles.
  • Audible, through immersion reading, when you own both the audiobook and the Kindle ebook of a Read & Listen title.

Speechify, Voice Dream Reader, Bookshare Reader and Dolphin EasyReader highlight the text as a synthetic voice reads it. Libby's narrated read-alongs, many with highlighted text, are aimed at children.

Do dyslexia fonts in reading apps help?

Not measurably, in the studies so far. Wery and Diliberto found OpenDyslexic gave elementary students with dyslexia "no improvement in reading rate or accuracy", and Kuster and colleagues found 170 children with dyslexia read no faster or more accurately in the Dyslexie font. Extra letter spacing has better evidence, so treat a dyslexia font as a comfort choice and try wider spacing too. HushBook pairs OpenDyslexic, Lexend, Atkinson Hyperlegible and Andika with adjustable spacing and color overlays; Libby offers OpenDyslexic in its ebook reader, and EasyReader offers dyslexia-friendly fonts and spacing. The studies are in do dyslexia fonts help.

Learning Ally: human-read textbooks, for students who qualify

Learning Ally offers "accessible audiobooks for adults with documented print disabilities" from a library of "80,000+ human-narrated titles."

  • Human-read audio with word-by-word highlighting on titles that include text
  • College textbooks, test prep and classic literature, including many STEM titles
  • Speed control, bookmarks, notes and an in-app dictionary

Price: $135 a year for an individual adult membership, as of October 30, 2026.

Catch: "Learning Ally membership is required to use this app," and Learning Ally must receive your proof of eligibility within 30 days of registration. Not every title shows text: Learning Ally has described its Classic Audio titles as "human-narrated with no highlighted text on the screen."

HushBook asks for no membership and no eligibility form, and every audiobook you add gets word-by-word text.

Website: learningally.org

Bookshare with Dolphin EasyReader: a text-to-speech library, for readers who qualify

Bookshare is an ebook library for people with reading barriers such as dyslexia, blindness, low vision and physical disabilities. Dolphin EasyReader is a free reading app that connects to Bookshare and about 50 other accessible libraries.

  • Over 1 million titles, with new books in the collection "the same day they are available on other mainstream book outlets"
  • Text-to-speech with highlighted text in Bookshare Reader and EasyReader
  • Dyslexia-friendly fonts, text and background colors, and letter and line spacing in EasyReader

Price: free for qualified US students; $79.99 a year for US adults. EasyReader is free, with Premium at $4.99 a month or $49.99 a year on the US App Store, as of October 30, 2026.

Catch: "The majority of Bookshare books use the text to speech voices built into the device," and the free EasyReader app downloads up to five titles at a time.

HushBook needs no proof of disability, and you hear the audiobook's human narrator, not a device voice.

Websites: bookshare.org and Dolphin EasyReader

Voice Dream says it turns "PDFs, articles, reports, and books into audio."

  • 36 free built-in iOS voices and more than 200 premium voices

Price: free trial for new users, then from $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year on the US App Store, as of October 30, 2026.

Catch: "A subscription is required to access the app" once the trial ends, and its site names only iPhone, iPad and Mac.

HushBook is free to download on iPhone, iPad and Android, with read-along free for every book you add.

Website: voicedream.com

Audible Read & Listen with Kindle: highlighted text, if you buy both editions

Audible's immersion reading lets listeners "follow along with highlighted text as they listen."

  • Read & Listen on more than 800,000 English and German titles with the badge
  • A built-in dictionary for English and Spanish titles
  • Switching between the Kindle ebook and the audiobook without losing your place

Price: Audible Standard $8.99 a month (one audiobook a month) or Premium $14.95 a month, as of October 30, 2026. The Kindle ebook is bought separately.

Catch: Audible's help center says "You will need both the audiobook and the Kindle eBook to access this feature." Its October 26, 2026 expansion to more countries did not change that wording.

HushBook makes the word-by-word text from the recording itself, so one audiobook file is all you need.

Libby: library audiobooks, with a library card

Libby is a free app for borrowing ebooks, audiobooks and magazines from your public library.

  • Free with a library card, with no late fees
  • Audiobooks at speeds from 0.6x to 3x
  • OpenDyslexic font in the ebook reader
  • Read-along ebooks with recorded narration, many with highlighted text

Price: free with a library card.

Catch: Libby offers its read-alongs to read "with your kids", and its audiobooks "can't be transferred to MP3 players or iPods", so they play only in Libby or at libbyapp.com. What you can borrow depends on your library.

HushBook highlights every word in books for adults that you already own, with a reading profile set up for dyslexia.

How we chose

We ranked apps for an adult with dyslexia who wants to read books, not only hear them. Our criteria, in order:

  1. Narration a person recorded, with each word highlighted
  2. Who can use it without proving a disability
  3. Price for an adult
  4. Reading controls such as fonts, spacing and speed
  5. Platforms

We read each app's official site, help center and US App Store listing on October 30, 2026, and took every price from those pages. HushBook is our app. We put it first because it is the one option here that combines human narration, word-by-word text and no eligibility step for books you already own. The rest are ordered by how close they come to that.
