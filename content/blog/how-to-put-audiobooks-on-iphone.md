# How to Put Audiobooks on iPhone: From a PC, a Mac or Without iTunes

It depends on where the audiobook comes from. Books you buy or borrow in an app, such as Apple Books, Audible or Libby, download inside that app. Open your library there and tap Download; there is nothing to transfer.

Your own audiobook files, such as MP3 or M4B, take two steps. First, get the file onto the iPhone: AirDrop it from a Mac, save it to iCloud Drive from a PC or Mac, copy it from a USB drive, or download it straight to the phone in Safari. Then open it in an app that plays audiobooks. Apple's own route into the Books app runs through a Mac: import the file into Books on the Mac, then sync it with the Finder.

If you'd rather not go through Books at all, **[HushBook](/)** imports MP3 and M4B files straight from the Files app and *highlights each word as the narrator reads it*. The steps for Apple, Audible, Libby and LibriVox below come from their own help pages, checked on October 28, 2026.

## How do you put audiobooks on an iPhone?

Pick the route by where the book comes from:

- **Bought in Apple Books.** Open Books and tap Library. Apple sorts what you buy into collections, including one called Audiobooks.
- **Audible.** Open the Audible app, find the title in your Library, tap the three-dot More menu and choose Download.
- **Borrowed in Libby.** It plays in the Libby app. Libby's help says its audiobooks "can't be transferred to MP3 players or iPods."
- **An MP3 or M4B file on a computer.** Move it with iCloud Drive, a USB drive or AirDrop, then import it into an audiobook app. From a Mac, you can also sync it into Books.
- **A free download, such as a LibriVox book.** Download it on the iPhone in Safari. It lands in the Files app, in Downloads.

Not sure which format to keep? M4B holds a whole book in one file with chapters, and MP3 plays almost anywhere. Our [M4B vs MP3](/blog/m4b-vs-mp3) guide compares them.

## How to put audiobooks on iPhone from a PC

On Windows 10 and later, Apple's sync tool is the Apple Devices app. Its user guide covers music, movies, TV shows, photos and files, and has no page for audiobooks. So from a PC, the dependable routes go through the iPhone's Files app.

**iCloud Drive.** It comes with your Apple Account.

1. Install iCloud for Windows, open it, and turn on iCloud Drive. It then appears in File Explorer's navigation pane.
2. Drag the audiobook file into iCloud Drive.
3. On the iPhone, open Files, tap Browse, then iCloud Drive. The file is there.
4. To keep it on the phone for offline listening, touch and hold it and tap Keep Downloaded.

Watch the space. Apple's free iCloud tier is 5 GB, and LibriVox says a whole-book zip can run from under 100 MB to more than 1 GB.

**A USB drive.** Copy the file to a USB drive or SD card on the PC, then connect the drive to the iPhone. Apple notes you might need a cable adapter. The drive appears in Files under Locations, and you copy the file across from there. No account and no internet needed, which helps with long books.

**A cloud service.** If you already use a storage service such as Dropbox or Box, upload the file on the PC. On the iPhone, open Files, tap Browse and tap the service's name below Locations.

**The Apple Devices app.** Connect the iPhone, select it in the sidebar, then select Files. You'll see the apps on your iPhone that support file sharing. Drag the audiobook onto one of them. Apple says "The files are immediately transferred to the device and are available using the app."

What about iTunes? Apple's iPhone guide points Windows 10 and later to Apple Devices, and keeps iTunes for Windows 7 or earlier. You also don't need the paid transfer tools that fill the search results for this question. Every route above is free.

## How to put audiobooks on iPhone from a Mac

A Mac gives you two choices: sync the book into Apple Books, or send the file to the iPhone.

**Sync it into Books.**

1. Open Books on the Mac, choose File > Import, select the audiobook and click Import. Apple says Books on Mac imports content you didn't get from its stores, "such as PDFs, mp3 audiobooks" and many EPUB books.
2. Connect the iPhone with a cable, open a Finder window and select the iPhone in the sidebar.
3. Click Audiobooks in the row near the top. Select "Sync audiobooks onto [device name]" for everything, or "Selected audiobooks" to choose.
4. Click Apply, then click Eject in the sidebar before you unplug.

Those steps come from Apple's guide for macOS Sequoia. Apple's newest Mac guide lists books, not audiobooks, among the content you can sync, so if your Mac shows no Audiobooks tab, check Books. After the first sync by cable, you can turn on syncing over Wi-Fi. One warning from Apple: with automatic syncing, if you delete a synced item from your Mac, "the deleted item is removed from your device the next time you sync."

**Send it with AirDrop.** In a Finder window, click AirDrop in the sidebar and drag the file onto your iPhone. If both devices use the same Apple Account, the iPhone accepts it automatically. Apple says some items arrive in the Files app on iPhone, and calls AirDrop "best for sharing a small selection of files."

**Use iCloud Drive.** Drag the file into iCloud Drive in the Finder, then find it in Files on the iPhone, as in the PC steps.

## How to put audiobooks on iPhone without iTunes

Every route above except the old iTunes one works without iTunes, and two need no computer at all:

- **Download it in Safari.** Apple's Files app keeps downloads in Browse > Downloads. If you don't see Downloads, tap iCloud Drive.
- **Get free classics from LibriVox.** Each LibriVox catalog page offers the whole book as a zip of 64kbps MP3 files, and LibriVox says "M4B formats are available for most of our books." On an iPhone the M4B is the tidy choice: one file, with chapters. If you take the zip, unzip it in Files: touch and hold it, then tap Uncompress.

Before downloading, our guide to [whether LibriVox is safe and legal](/blog/is-librivox-safe-and-legal) covers what you're getting.

## How to add audiobooks to Apple Books

**To buy one,** open Books, tap Audiobooks or Search, choose a title and tap Buy, or Get for a free title. It is saved in your Library.

**To add your own file,** go through a Mac, as above. Apple's iPhone guide for Books covers buying audiobooks, listening, and opening PDFs from other apps, and has no step for adding an audio file on the iPhone itself. A 2024 test by ProVideo Coalition found the same. When the author opened or downloaded a DRM-free M4B on an iPhone, "Nothing happens," and Books for iPhone "has no Import or Open function either." The same file opened straight into Books on a Mac.

Some books won't go into Books at all. Apple says "Some content (such as ACSM, AZW, and ODM files) can only be opened in the associated third-party app." Apple Books also "isn't available in all countries or regions."

## Where do audiobooks go on iPhone?

It depends on how they got there:

- **Bought in Apple Books, or synced from a Mac:** in the Books app. Tap Library, then the collections button, then Audiobooks. In the car, CarPlay plays them from its Audiobooks app.
- **Audible:** in the Audible app's Library. Audible's help describes downloading titles to its app or to a computer, not to Files or Books.
- **Libby:** in the Libby app.
- **Files you downloaded or AirDropped:** in the Files app, under Downloads or Recents. They stay there as plain files.

That last group is where most people get stuck. The book is on the phone, but Files is a file manager, not an audiobook player. **HushBook** turns that file into a book with one import, with *chapters, cover art and metadata* brought along automatically.

## How to listen to downloaded audiobooks on iPhone

**In Apple Books,** tap the cover to play. Apple lists the controls: skip forward or back, a chapter list, a speed dial behind the 1x button, a sleep timer and a mini-player. Apple notes that "Some audiobooks refer to chapters as tracks, or don't define chapters."

**In Audible or Libby,** play the book in that app's own player.

**For an MP3 or M4B in Files,** open it in an audiobook app. In **HushBook**, importing your own files is *free*:

1. Open the Library and tap the plus button, Import audiobook.
2. Tap Choose an audio file and pick it in Files, from iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, Downloads or a connected drive.
3. Check the title, author and cover on the Book info screen, then save.

Got a book that comes as a folder of MP3 parts? Select them all at once, and **HushBook** keeps them as *one book, one chapter per file*, in file-name order. Our guide to [playing M4B files](/blog/how-to-play-m4b-files) covers other players too.

## Why won't my audiobook show up on iPhone?

- **It's copy-protected.** Audible books stay in the Audible app and Libby loans stay in Libby. Apple warns that transferring or syncing "may be restricted" for "files protected by copyrights."
- **It's still zipped.** A LibriVox zip is one archive, not a book. Touch and hold it in Files and tap Uncompress.
- **It's still in iCloud.** Touch and hold the file in Files and tap Keep Downloaded so it stays on the phone. Apple says that if Keep Downloaded doesn't appear, "the file is already on your iPhone."
- **You're looking in Books for a file you only copied.** Books shows what you bought or synced from a Mac. A file you downloaded or AirDropped waits in Files until you import it into an app.
- **The sync never ran.** In the Finder, nothing moves until you click Apply.

## What we could not confirm

- Whether the Apple Devices app on Windows can sync audiobooks into Books. Its guide has no page for it, and we did not test it.
- The name of the Finder's audiobook tab on the newest macOS. Apple's Sequoia guide calls it Audiobooks; the newest guide lists books.
- Exactly where AirDrop saves an audio file on iPhone. Apple says only that "some items may be saved in the Files app."
