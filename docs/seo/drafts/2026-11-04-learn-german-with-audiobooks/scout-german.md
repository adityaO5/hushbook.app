## Target

- **Slug:** `learn-german-with-audiobooks`
- **H1 (61 chars):** How to Learn German With Audiobooks (and Free Ones With Text)
- **Answer-first opening sentence:** Audiobooks help you learn German when you can follow the words: start with short stories written for learners at about A2, keep the German text in front of you while you listen, and replay each chapter until it makes sense without the text.
- **Second beat (the German reason for the text):** German often holds back the word that carries the meaning (the verb, or the prefix of a separable verb) until the end of the clause, so by ear you have to keep the whole sentence in your head, and the text shows you where it lands.

3. **Why is spoken German so hard to follow?** Four German-specific reasons, one short paragraph or list item each:
   - Separable verbs: *Ich rufe dich morgen an.* The prefix that changes the meaning comes last.
   - Verb-final subordinate clauses: *…weil ich das Buch gestern gekauft habe.* The main verb arrives at the end.
   - Long compounds: *Lebensmittelgeschäft*, *Haftpflichtversicherung*. Heard as one long word, easier to read.
   - Cases: *der/den/dem/des* and adjective endings are unstressed and blur in speech (*-en*, *-em*, *-er*), while the text keeps them distinct.
   - Optional fifth: spoken reductions (*haben wir* sounding like *hamma*, *ich hab's*, *nich*).
   - FSI difficulty number to verify: German listed at 36 weeks / 900 class hours (Category II) vs Spanish 24 to 30 weeks.
4. **What are the best German audiobooks for beginners?** H3s for A1 and A2. Learner short-story series (Dino lernt Deutsch / Learn German with Stories, Olly Richards), Readle, DW for slow audio. Matches the level cluster (a1, a2, beginners, easy).
