# Best Audiobook Version of The Count of Monte Cristo: Every Unabridged Edition Compared

The best paid *Count of Monte Cristo* audiobook is John Lee's Blackstone recording, winner of the 2009 Audie Award for male solo narration.

John Lee's unabridged recording runs 46 hrs and 56 mins on Audible and is sold on Audible, Apple Books and Downpour. The best free one is David Clarke's solo LibriVox recording: one voice for all 117 chapters, 54 hours 16 minutes, in the public domain. An audiobook listed on the Robin Buss translation's page at Penguin UK is expected on February 11, 2027; Audible calls it unabridged, and no narrator or length has been announced. If you want a second paid option, Bill Homewood's Naxos recording runs 52 hours 41 minutes. We have not listened to the more than 850 hours these 17 recordings add up to, so our picks rest on things you can check: an award, the publisher, one voice or many, the running time, where each is sold and whether you keep a file. Play the free sample of John Lee's recording and the first chapter of David Clarke's before you choose. Every edition fact below was read from the publisher's or store's own page on November 10, 2026.

## Which Count of Monte Cristo audiobook is best?

John Lee for a paid copy, David Clarke for a free one, and Bill Homewood as the paid runner-up.

### Best paid: John Lee (Blackstone, 2008)

The Audio Publishers Association's 2009 Audie Awards page lists John Lee's recording as the winner for solo narration, male: "The Count of Monte Cristo by Alexandre Dumas, Narrated by John Lee (Blackstone Audio, Inc.)". That award is the one independent judgment on any of these recordings we could verify. At 46 hrs and 56 mins on Audible, it is also the shortest of our three picks. Downpour sells it as a download, on CD and on MP3-CD.

### Best free: David Clarke (LibriVox, 2013)

David Clarke reads every chapter of LibriVox's third recording, catalogued on August 9, 2013, at 54:16:11. It is public domain, so you can stream it or download the MP3 or M4B files and keep them, with no copy protection. It is also the only English recording on LibriVox with a single voice from start to finish.

### Runner-up: Bill Homewood (Naxos AudioBooks, 2010)

Bill Homewood's unabridged recording runs 52h 41m on the Naxos page, across 41 CDs in the booklet's count (52:43:38). It is sold on Audible, Apple Books and Google Play, and the Naxos page points to Downpour for the CD and links the recording to Spotify. Naxos also publishes a 2-hour 38-minute abridgement read by Bill Homewood, a short way to hear his reading before you commit to 52 hours.

## Which translation does each Count of Monte Cristo audiobook use?

Only the three LibriVox recordings say: they read Project Gutenberg eBook #1184, and no commercial audiobook page we read names a translation or a translator.

None of the store or publisher pages we read for the editions above names a translator. Project Gutenberg's own record for eBook #1184 does not name one either: it credits "Anonymous Project Gutenberg Volunteers, Dan Muller and David Widger", with a release date of January 1, 1998.

Two ways to narrow it down, both our own observations rather than publisher statements:

- **The Naxos booklet.** The track cue lines in the booklet for Bill Homewood's recording match the Gutenberg #1184 wording word for word, for example "Without her captain!" cried Dantès, from the opening chapters. That suggests Bill Homewood reads the same text as LibriVox, but Naxos does not say so.
- **The opening line.** You can test a sample yourself. Gutenberg #1184 opens: "On the 24th of February, 1815, the look-out at Notre-Dame de la Garde signalled the three-master, the *Pharaon* from Smyrna, Trieste, and Naples." Other English texts can word it differently. The print excerpt on the Penguin Random House page that also sells Richard Matthews's recording opens "ON FEBRUARY 24, 1815, the watchtower at Marseilles signaled". That excerpt comes from a print edition, so it does not tell you what the recording reads.

Robin Buss's translation is published by Penguin Classics, which describes it this way: "Robin Buss's lively translation is complete and unabridged, and remains faithful to the style of Dumas's original."

**Is there a Robin Buss audiobook?** Not yet, as far as Penguin's pages show. On November 10, 2026, Penguin UK listed an "Audio Download" on the Buss translation's page, ISBN 9781837316199, with a publication date of February 11, 2027 and a price of £16.00, marked "(P) Penguin Audio 2027". That page credits "Robin Buss (Introducer)". It names no translator, no narrator and no length. Audible US lists the same recording for February 11, 2027 as an "Unabridged Audiobook" with length "Not Yet Known". Penguin UK's pre-order links go to Apple Books, Bookshop.org, Hive, Kobo, Libro.fm and xigxag. Because it sits on the Buss translation's page, it is expected to be the Buss text, but no page states that yet.

## Should you get the abridged or unabridged Count of Monte Cristo audiobook?

## Is The Count of Monte Cristo hard to follow as an audiobook?

The length and the number of names are the hard part on audio, because you can't flip back a page to check who someone is.

Reading and listening both work if you plan for that. The BnF, France's national library, records that Dumas's novel ran as a serial in the *Journal des Débats* from August 28, 1844 to January 15, 1846, in three runs, and was then issued in 18 volumes in 1845 and 1846. The English text on Project Gutenberg has 117 chapters in five volumes, about 460,000 words.

Here is who you meet first, with the chapter where each name first appears in Project Gutenberg #1184. These lines give nothing away.

Other names change too. Fernand becomes the Count de Morcerf (named in chapter 27), and Danglars is a baron by chapter 27.

The payoff is in lines like this one, from the count's letter in chapter 117: "There is neither happiness nor misery in the world; there is only the comparison of one state with another, nothing more." The same letter sums up all human wisdom in two words: "Wait and hope."

On audio, the hard part is remembering who someone is when they come back many chapters later, sometimes under a new name. ## What the evidence does not show

- **Accents.** No publisher, store or narrator page we reached states a narrator's accent.
- **Translations.** No commercial edition names its translation. The Naxos match with Gutenberg #1184 is our own comparison.
- **Sound.** We did not rank the recordings by listening, and we describe no narrator's voice.
- **Your country.** Availability varies by region. The Naxos site blocked direct purchase in our region, and we could not check Libro.fm, Kobo, Chirp or Spotify listings for this book.
- **The 2027 recording.** Narrator, length and which text Penguin Audio's recording reads were not announced on November 10, 2026.
- **AI and small-label listings.** We excluded them rather than test them, so some may be complete.
