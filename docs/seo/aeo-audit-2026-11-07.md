# AEO audit: hushbook.app blog, 2026-11-07

Goal: get blog posts cited by ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and Google AI Overviews, not only ranked on Google.

Scope: 4 posts (`audiobooks-with-captions`, `learn-spanish-with-audiobooks`, `how-to-take-notes-on-audiobooks`, `how-to-focus-on-audiobooks`), the blog template (`scripts/build-blog.js`), site-level files (`robots.txt`, `llms.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `_headers`, `src/`, `scripts/indexnow-ping.js`), live fetches and a Bing-like index probe. Read-only: nothing in the repo was changed except this file.

Owner decisions respected: `llms.txt` keeps plain "free" (no Pro wording), no Play data-safety work, no Audiobookshelf angle.

### What the top scorer has that the others lack

By the script's rules, `learn-spanish-with-audiobooks` wins on Experience (64) because it has the most sentences that start "In 20XX, ..." (4 "dated example" hits) and two uses of "result", plus the most table rows (19). The three long posts sit within 3 points of each other, and those gaps come from regex artifacts (see below). The real split is long and heavily sourced (78 to 85) against short with one source (40 markdown, 73 HTML). What the long posts have and the focus post lacks, in the script's terms:

- 34 to 55 outbound links (focus: 1) and 9 to 20 DOI links (focus: 0).
- 3,400+ words (focus: 978).
- Bullet lists and pipe tables (focus: 1 bullet, 0 tables; its steps are H3s, which the list counter ignores).
- "In 2024, X found" phrasing (focus says "In a 2013 study", which the dated-example regex misses).

### Why the score does not predict citation here

The post that scores lowest is the only one with proven AI citation. `how-to-focus-on-audiobooks` reached #7 on Google for "how to focus on audiobooks" and was the first AI Overview source within days of publishing (weekly plan 2026-10-20), then averaged position 5.3 on 248 impressions (weekly plan 2026-10-26). The script counts volume, and several of its patterns are broken:

- **Expertise is 100% false positives.** The credential regex includes `do`, so every "do" in the text counts as a credential. All 4 to 11 "credential" hits in each post are the verb "do".
- **Percentages never match.** `\d+%\b` needs a word character after `%`, so "90% of the words" scores 0. Same bug for `$15`.
- **Authority and Trust are link counts.** Every `https://` adds points, including our own CTA and font URLs in the HTML.
- **author_byline is 0 everywhere**, which is accurate: no post shows an author name (see section 2).

Use the script as a smoke test for missing schema or a missing source list. Do not chase its composite.

Features of the proven post, in order of how likely they mattered:

1. **One query, one page.** Title and H1 start with the exact query, and every section answers it. The newer posts answer 9 to 12 different questions each. That helps long-tail coverage but dilutes the page for its head query.
2. **A one-line answer under a question H2.** "Why is it so hard to focus on audiobooks?" is answered with "Because listening asks less of your body than reading does." (10 words), then one complete study: year, journal, authors, n = 211, result.
3. **Numbered steps as H3s** ("### 1. Give your hands something simple to do"). This is the shape AI Overviews lift for "how to" queries.
4. **Short answer units.** Takeaways of 22 to 32 words and FAQ answers of 23 to 36 words. Answer engines quote 1 to 3 sentences. The new posts' FAQ answers run to 83 words.
5. **Takeaways box first.** The rendered page shows "Key takeaways" before the prose, so the answer leads even though the prose opens with a scene.

Caveats: n = 1, the newer posts are 3 to 6 days old, and most of the 11 inbound links came after the win. The newer posts already do points 2 and 5 well, and their sourcing is stronger. They lose on points 1 and 4.

## 2. Machine-readability of a post (`blog/how-to-focus-on-audiobooks.html`, `scripts/build-blog.js`)

### Present and correct

- Static HTML with the full text in the first response. No JS needed to read content. `<main>` and `<article>` wrap the post.
- JSON-LD `@graph`: **BlogPosting** (headline, description, `abstract` = takeaways, datePublished, dateModified, wordCount, articleSection, keywords, inLanguage, mainEntityOfPage, isPartOf Blog, author, publisher, about, speakable), **FAQPage** (6 Q&A), **BreadcrumbList**.
- **Person** author with `@id https://hushbook.app/about#rakesh-aditya`, jobTitle, image and sameAs (personal site, LinkedIn). It matches the Person and Organization `@id`s on `about.html` and `index.html`.
- Visible dates: "Published October 15, 2026", plus "Updated" when `dateModified` differs. `article:published_time` and `article:modified_time` meta.
- Canonical, `og:*`, `twitter:*`, `max-snippet:-1`. hreflang is only `en` + `x-default` pointing at itself (no noise; `blog-pages.test.js` enforces it).
- **Key takeaways** box above the prose (this is the "key facts" block), and `speakable` points at it. Hidden only on `layout: 'essay'` posts.
- FAQs are visible text in `<details><summary>`, not only schema.
- Sources list with outbound links. Newer posts use DOI links.
- Clean heading order: one H1, H2 sections, H3 steps.
- Consistent entity naming: "HushBook" everywhere in prose; lowercase only inside URLs.
- Weight: 26 to 60 KB HTML (8 to 18 KB gzip). Render-blocking is Google Fonts CSS, `alternatives.css` (11 KB) and `blog.css` (3.5 KB); gtag is async. None of it matters to fetchers that do not render.

- **Cloudflare zone settings are active and are not in the repo:**

## 6. AEO checklist for a new post

1. Title and H1 start with the exact query; `metaTitle` is 60 characters or fewer.
2. Takeaways: 4 or 5 points, 20 to 35 words each, every one stated in the body. They are the first text engines see.
3. First 200 words: sentence one answers the title in under 25 words. The next sentences give the deciding fact and what the guide covers. No scene before the answer.
4. H2s copy People Also Ask wording as questions. The first line under each is a one-sentence answer ("Partly.", "Because listening asks less of your body than reading does."), then the evidence.
5. "How to" posts: numbered H3 steps ("### 1. Rewind the moment you notice"), 2 to 4 sentences each.
6. Each section stands alone: name the subject ("audiobook captions", "HushBook") instead of "it" or "the app", so a lifted chunk still makes sense.
7. Fact density: at least one sourced number per H2, about 3 to 8 per 1,000 words. Never a number without its source.
8. Cite in the sentence: "In a 2013 study of 211 students, Varao Sousa, Carriere and Smilek found..." (year, n, authors, finding). If n is not given, say so. List it under `## Sources` as `[Authors (Year), "Title", Journal](https://doi.org/...)`.
9. Service and app facts carry their check date in the text ("read from Libby's help pages on November 4, 2026"). One date format: November 4, 2026.
10. Entities: "HushBook" in exact case; official names for others (Audible, Libby, hoopla, Apple Books, Spotify, LibriVox).
11. FAQ: 5 to 7 questions as people type them, covering variants the H2s miss. Answers 40 words or fewer; the first word is yes, no or a number.
12. Honesty: a "What the evidence does not show" section, and one sentence on what HushBook cannot do when it matters (protected files, speech-recognition errors on names, English-only dictionary). Product claims match the app (long-press a line to play from there; tap-to-seek is off by default).
13. HushBook appears once, value-first, where the pain is felt, plus the `cta` field. Say "we built the app" when recommending it.
14. Avoid: em dashes, filler openers, "studies show" without naming the study, unsourced superlatives, FAQ answers over 40 words, and bumping `dateModified` for link-only edits.
15. After deploy: IndexNow for the new URL (until fix 1 automates it), then request indexing in Search Console.
